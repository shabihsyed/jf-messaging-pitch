JF Messaging Narrative

More code isn’t more value.

Almost every engineering team now runs AI coding agents. Code throughput has roughly doubled. And yet fewer than half of engineering leaders can say what any of it is worth. This manifesto is the story Jellyfish tells to close that gap, in the order a first call should tell it.

The shift

The bottleneck moved, and the old metrics can’t see it.

AI triples the code moving through the pipeline. It also creates new bottlenecks in review and CI, new costs in tokens and seats, and new questions from finance and the board. Agent-written code passes checks by default and more of it merges with AI as the only reviewer, so the one checkpoint everyone relied on, code review, has quietly stopped working as the quality gate. Metrics built for human-only teams can’t tell you which changes are helping.

Ask four people in the same org and you get four answers. The engineer says they’re shipping 3x faster. The manager says reviews are piling up and they can’t tell what the agent did. The VP asks whether the spend is paying off. The CTO asks whether quality is holding. Same tools, four expectations, no shared number.

What Jellyfish is

The Agentic Engineering Intelligence and AI Impact Platform.

For engineering teams running an AI-native SDLC who can’t prove what agents return, Jellyfish shows how every agent, tool and team performs, ties it to delivery, quality and cost, and benchmarks it against 1,000+ organizations. Unlike metrics built for human-only teams, it’s built for human + agent work: people using AI and fully autonomous agents side by side, in the metrics leadership already trusts.

The product is organized around the three questions every engineering leader gets asked today. Where do we stand? Are we transforming? What is it worth? We sell it as a journey with three stops: Adopt, Transform, Thrive.

Stop one · Adopt

Maximize your AI coding agents’ ROI.

Everyone has adopted AI. No one can prove AI Impact. Ninety percent of teams already run coding agents, so this stop is about measuring what’s already there, not rolling it out. Jellyfish answers where do I stand? in the first weeks: any metric leadership already trusts, split by human contributors and autonomous agents; agents compared on outcomes instead of seats; power users found and their habits spread; and all of it benchmarked against 1,000+ orgs. When a number moves, you ask the Assistant why and get the likely cause.

LastPass rolled Claude to 100% of 200 engineers and tracked it in Jellyfish. The issue lifecycle shortened 30–40% and the best AI-adopting teams delivered 2–3x faster. Loadsmart used the same evidence to choose Cursor over Copilot when cycle times flattened.

Stop two · Transform

Run the AI-native SDLC.

Teams feel busier, not faster. The bottleneck moved. Before AI, writing code was the constraint. With agents, code doubles and the constraint moves downstream to review and CI, so features rise only 40–50%. Just using AI isn’t sufficient anymore; the practices behind the output are what scale. Over the first two quarters Jellyfish answers am I transforming? by showing where the work really flows now, which skills and workflows your fastest teams use, and whether heavy adopters of a practice actually rework less and ship sooner than light ones. Early signals in weeks, instead of waiting for word of mouth.

Proof
At Loadsmart, 86% of code is AI-generated and review had become 25% of delivery time. They paired AI coding with AI review, measured it in Jellyfish, and cut review delay to under 2% while feature velocity doubled. taskrabbit saw a 50% drop in issue cycle time and 2x deploys after adopting Augment.
Research
Tenfold token use buys about 2.2x the merged PRs; gains taper at the top. PRs merged with AI as the only reviewer: 0.5% at the median company, 9% at lead adopters.

Stop three · Thrive

Human + agent teams that move the business.

AI cost has exploded, and the CFO wants to know what you’re getting for it. Token spend per merged PR is up 60x in twelve months, and the 2027 budget is being set now. By the next planning cycle Jellyfish answers what is it worth? with three questions finance will recognize. What are we spending, by vendor, model and team, next to total R&D cost? What are we spending it on, mapped to the same investment categories used for people’s effort? And what are we getting for it, as cost per merged PR split human versus AI, with capacity gains told apart from hiring? Alongside the money, the people: delivery metrics next to developer surveys so speed gains don’t hide burnout, and one number for the board on a fixed cadence.

What comes next is Agent Observatory, in Early Access from December 1. Quality is decided in the agent session before a PR is ever opened, so Jellyfish captures every session from every agent, links it to its commits and PR, scores it against the original intent, and puts coaching for the author and a heads-up for the reviewer right on the PR. Prompts analyzed, never shown.

Who we sell to

Six people, three conversations.

The ideal customer is an engineering org of 200 to 5,000 engineers that has already rolled out Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot or Augment and now has to prove what the rollout bought, often to a board or a private-equity partner. Inside that org the story lands differently depending on who is in the room.

CTO / CPTO
Lead with Thrive, then Adopt
Adoption looks good but they can’t prove what AI is buying, and the board asks before 2027 budgets close. They want one shared view of human and agent work and an answer for the board. Keep it strategic; skip feature detail.
VP / Head of Eng
Lead with Adopt and Transform
They can’t separate agent-driven velocity from hidden risk. Show agent activity tied to delivery, quality and cost. Never offer adoption stats as proof.
CFO / Finance
Lead with Thrive
Token bills grow with no link to output. Talk cost per outcome, unit economics and spend mapped to work. No engineering jargon.
Platform / DevEx
Lead with Transform
2x code moved the bottleneck to review and CI, code review is no longer the gate, and proof dies at the next tool migration. Show the real bottleneck and the practices that work. Never speed without quality.
Eng Manager / TPM
Lead with Adopt and Transform
No baseline, and reviews pile up with no idea what the agent did. Benchmarks, skills taking hold, and the Assistant. Avoid vague productivity claims.
Engineer (IC)
Lead with Adopt
Shipping 3x faster but the metrics count what they typed, and measurement feels like surveillance. Credit for agent-directed work and one thing to try per session. Never individual rankings.
